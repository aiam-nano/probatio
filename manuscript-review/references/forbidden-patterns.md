# Forbidden patterns

Governs **the report you write**. It applies to the manuscript only as a secondary pass, with the caution set out at the end. It also governs this repository's own prose; see the last section.

## Why this exists

A review that reads as machine-generated is discounted, however good its content. The report has to read as though a working scientist wrote it.

## Banned vocabulary

**Inflation.** Innovative, pioneering, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art used as praise rather than as a named benchmark, remarkable, unprecedented, breakthrough, superior, excel, surpass, profound, compelling, robust used as a compliment rather than a tested property, powerful, seamless, elegant.

**Filler and transition tics.** Notably, importantly, it is worth noting, it should be noted, crucially, fundamentally, essentially, at its core, in essence, ultimately, moreover, furthermore in excess, delve, leverage as a verb, underscore, highlight meaning emphasise, showcase, pave the way for, holds promise for, opens new avenues, sheds light on, plays a crucial role, serves as a testament.

**Hollow verbs.** Encompass, entail, harness, unlock, foster, facilitate where allow or let is meant, utilise where use is meant.

**Assessment-free adjectives.** Comprehensive, thorough, rigorous, systematic, holistic, nuanced, applied to the manuscript without saying what makes it so.

## Structural tells

**No em-dashes as sentence connectors.** Use a comma, a semicolon, a colon, or two sentences. Em-dash density is the single most recognisable rhythm of machine prose.

**No "not X, but Y" constructions.** State Y.

**No habitual tricolon.** Three parallel items in a row, repeatedly, is a tell. Vary the shape of sentences.

**No rhetorical question answered in the next sentence.**

**No participial pileup at the head of a sentence.** "Building on this, leveraging the framework, the authors demonstrate..."

**No closing sentence that restates the paragraph just written.** A paragraph ends when its point is made.

**No manufactured balance.** Do not pair every criticism with a compensating compliment.

**No hedge-stacking.** "It may potentially be somewhat unclear whether" says nothing. Commit to the assessment.

## Positive requirements

Vary sentence length; a run of same-length sentences is itself a tell. Prefer concrete nouns to abstractions. Name things specifically: "Table 2" rather than "the results table", "the crosslinking step" rather than "the process". Use the active voice where the actor matters. Let an occasional short sentence land a point.

## Applying this to the manuscript

A vocabulary scan of the manuscript can be a useful secondary pass. Inflated language in a paper often marks thin evidence, because the words cluster where the support is weakest, which makes them a navigational aid.

Two constraints on reporting it.

**Do not frame it as AI detection.** These markers do not establish machine authorship. A rushed human produces all of them; a carefully revised assisted draft produces none. What they measure is weak writing. Report the weak writing.

**A clean scan licenses nothing.** Every one of these words can be removed by find-and-replace in ten minutes without touching a line of reasoning. The scan is informative when it fires and silent when it passes. Never report that the language checks came back clean, because the instrument cannot issue that certificate. The same principle governs the embedded-instruction scan in `untrusted-input.md`.

Report inflated language as a minor finding, aggregated rather than instance by instance, unless it clusters around a specific weak claim. Then the finding is the weak claim, and the language is what led you to it.

## This repository's own prose

Everything in this repository is held to the standard above. A landing page that reads as machine-written, for a skill whose selling point is that its output does not, is a problem a reader notices in the first paragraph.
